GearOut lets a media program lend cameras, recorders, lights, and kits to students by the course. Built at the desk it runs.

GearOut started in a university equipment room, where a few hundred students borrow gear every week and one counter keeps track of all of it. It runs the equipment desk at the university where it was built. GearOut 2.0 is the version made for other institutions: one codebase, one stack per institution, with your department's name, color, pickup blocks, and sign-in method set by an installer rather than by a developer.

Every installation is one stack for one institution, and student records stay on that stack. Nothing reports back to us, there is no analytics service behind the pages, and no AI feature touches student data, because there is no AI feature.

Four people have to like it.

The student on a phone, the person at the counter, the chair who signs the purchase, and the IT office that reads the security packet. GearOut was built for all four, and the desk staff are the ones who decide whether it stays.

The student on a phone
Reserves a camera kit between classes, gets a six-letter pickup code and a QR pass that opens without a signal, and gets the three emails that matter: the confirmation, a reminder at 5:00 PM the day before, and a waitlist offer when a slot opens.
The desk
Scans in either direction. A gear label or a student's pass resolves to a check-out or a check-in with one button, and the board shows who has what, what is overdue, and what is due today. Renewals, damage reports, and fines are one press away.
The department
Eligibility follows the course. An instructor launches GearOut from the course in the learning management system and the roster syncs through LTI 1.3; a CSV upload with a preview is the fallback. The department sets the rules: pickup blocks, a weekly limit of three time slots per student, late fees, holds, and waitlists.
Campus IT and the accessibility office
Sign-in through the campus identity provider (Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, or a SAML 2.0 provider through SSOReady). PostgreSQL row-level security, secrets encrypted at rest, TLS in front, and a stack that fits in 2 vCPU and 4 GB of RAM. A VPAT 2.4 report, HECVAT 4 answers, a security summary, and a hosting and data-flow statement are in preparation for the review that happens before anyone signs.

How it works, in four steps.

  1. Connect a course.

    An instructor launches GearOut from the course in Blackboard or another LTI 1.3 platform, and the roster syncs through Names and Role Provisioning Services. Every enrolled student is now eligible. There is no directory sync with central IT; a CSV roster upload with a preview is the fallback.

  2. The student reserves.

    From a phone: open the item, tap the blocks on the calendar, confirm from the bar at the bottom of the screen. The confirmation shows a six-letter pickup code and a QR pass, which the student can save for offline. Three time slots a week, and several items in one block count as one slot.

  3. The desk scans.

    The student shows the pass, or the staff member scans the label on the gear. The scanner works out the direction: a confirmed reservation checks out, a checked-out item checks in. One button either way, and the scanner re-arms for the next person.

  4. The gear comes back, and the records keep themselves.

    Returns are scanned the same way. Overdue items, late fees, holds, renewals, damage reports, and waitlist offers follow the rules the department set, and reports on utilization, overdue items, fines, and use per course export as CSV.

A walk through a sample desk.

This walk-through uses a sample department and made-up gear. No real desk's data appears on this site.

Monday, 8:40 AM

A student in an introductory video course opens the department's checkout site from the link in the syllabus and signs in with the school account. The dashboard shows two of three weekly slots still free. They search for the interview kit, see Tuesday's 9:00 to 12:00 block in green, tap it, and confirm. The confirmation shows a QR pass and the pickup code KQ7M2P, and they tap Save pass for offline.

Tuesday, 9:05 AM

At the counter, the staff member has Scan open on the desk computer. The student holds up the phone. The card reads Ready to check out with the student's name, the kit, and the window, and one press of Check out does it. A confirmation names the student and the due time, and the scanner re-arms for the next person in line.

Tuesday, 11:50 AM

The kit comes back. The staff member scans the label on the case, the card reads Ready to check in, and one press completes the reservation. Had it come back at 1:30 PM, the late fee the department set would have been recorded against the student's account, and a hold, if the department uses holds, would stop the next booking until the fee is settled.

Wednesday

The chair asks how busy the desk was last week. Reports, filtered to the course, gives the utilization and overdue numbers, and the CSV goes into the budget request.

Questions a buyer asks.

How many institutions use it?
One, the university where it was built. GearOut 2.0 is the version built for other institutions.
Do you have SOC 2?
No. GearOut is built so it does not need to hold your data: the self-hosted options keep every record on your stack. The managed option is SIM DAD running one stack for you, never a shared database.
Is there a VPAT?
A VPAT 2.4 report for GearOut 2.0 is in preparation, covering every student and staff screen.
Does it work with our identity provider?
Microsoft Entra ID and Google Workspace connect directly. SAML 2.0 providers such as Shibboleth, ADFS, and Okta connect through the SSOReady broker. Your identity team registers one redirect URI, which the setup page prints for them.
Does it work with our LMS?
Any LTI 1.3 platform, with roster sync through Names and Role Provisioning Services. The desk it runs today launches from Blackboard. A CSV roster upload with a preview is the fallback.
Is there a mobile app?
GearOut is an installable web app. Students add it to the home screen, the pass opens offline, and there is no second app-store surface to keep accessible.
What if SIM DAD goes away?
Your stack keeps running. It does not call home.
Can we buy it on a card?
Starter and Department, with either provisioning fee, stay under common purchasing-card thresholds.
